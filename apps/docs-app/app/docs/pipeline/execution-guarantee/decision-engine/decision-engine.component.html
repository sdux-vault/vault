Decision Engine & Arbitrator

The Decision Engine and Arbitrator evaluate incoming state resolution requests forwarded by the Conductor. Together they determine whether a request is permitted, rejected, or deferred — producing a normalized execution directive for the Orchestrator within a .

The Decision Engine coordinates registered Controllers and delegates vote aggregation to the Arbitrator. The Arbitrator applies deterministic priority rules to produce exactly one outcome per attempt.

Responsibility

The Decision Engine and Arbitrator occupy the control layer between the Conductor and the Orchestrator. Their combined responsibilities are:

  • Attempt evaluation — Each state resolution attempt is submitted to all registered Controllers for evaluation.
  • Vote aggregation — Controller responses are normalized and aggregated by the Arbitrator using deterministic priority rules.
  • Decision production — A single outcome is produced for every attempt: Abstain, Deny, or Abort.
  • Lifecycle notification — Controllers are notified of Pipeline success, failure, and finalization events for observational purposes.

The Decision Engine does not modify State, does not participate in Pipeline stage traversal, and does not enqueue or dequeue attempts. The Arbitrator does not maintain execution history or internal state between evaluations.

Controller Evaluation

When the Conductor submits an attempt, the Decision Engine dispatches an evaluation message to all registered Controllers. Each Controller produces a vote indicating whether the attempt should proceed.

Controllers registered with a participate in every replaceState() or Merge attempt evaluation. They may implement policies such as rate limiting, circuit breakers, concurrency gates, or approval workflows.

Controllers are evaluated concurrently. All votes are collected before the Arbitrator produces its decision. A Controller that fails during evaluation is treated as a Deny vote.
Arbitration

The Arbitrator receives the collected votes and applies priority rules to produce a single decision. The priority order is fixed and deterministic:

Priority Vote Effect
1 (highest) Abort The attempt is permanently rejected. No Pipeline execution occurs.
2 Deny The attempt is blocked but may be re-evaluated when conditions change.
3 (lowest) Abstain No objection. The attempt proceeds to the Orchestrator for Pipeline execution.

If no Controllers are registered, the Arbitrator treats the evaluation as a unanimous Abstain and the attempt proceeds unconditionally.

Lifecycle Notifications

After Pipeline execution completes, the Decision Engine notifies registered Controllers of the result. These notifications are observational — they do not participate in arbitration or affect the decision for subsequent attempts.

Notification Trigger
Success Pipeline execution completed and State was committed successfully.
Failure Pipeline execution failed and the Error was committed to the State Snapshot.
Finalize The trace associated with the attempt has fully completed and resources may be released.

Controllers may use lifecycle notifications to update internal counters, release concurrency tokens, or adjust future vote behavior. A Controller that throws during notification is silently ignored.
