Trademark Usage

This page explains how names, logos, and brand assets may be used in open-source projects, documentation, integrations, and commercial products.

Trademarks exist to prevent confusion about what is official, endorsed, or maintained by the project. They are separate from software licenses and apply regardless of whether the underlying code is open source.

What This Policy Covers
↑ top

This policy applies to the use of trademarks, including product names, project names, slogans, and logos.

It does not change or replace any software license. Licenses govern how code may be used; trademarks govern how names and branding may be used.

Permitted Usage
↑ top

You may use trademarks in the following ways without prior approval:

  • Accurate references to software in documentation or tutorials
  • Statements such as “Built with ” or “Compatible with ”
  • Open-source integrations that clearly identify themselves as third-party
  • Academic, educational, or editorial references

All permitted usage must avoid implying official endorsement or ownership.

Restricted Usage
↑ top

The following uses are not permitted without explicit written permission:

  • Using names or logos as part of your own product or company name
  • Distributing modified versions labeled as official releases
  • Implying endorsement, partnership, or certification where none exists
  • Using branding in a way that causes confusion
Logos & Visual Assets
↑ top

The logo and related visual assets are protected brand assets.

Logos may not be modified, recolored, or incorporated into derivative marks without permission. Use of logos in commercial products or marketing materials requires approval.

Open Source & Trademarks
↑ top

Open-source licenses grant rights to use, modify, and distribute code. They do not grant rights to use trademarks.

Forks and derivative projects must use distinct names and branding to avoid confusion.

Enforcement & Questions
↑ top

Trademark enforcement is intended to prevent confusion, not to restrict legitimate open-source or community use.

If you are unsure whether a particular use is permitted, please reach out before publishing or distributing materials.
